# Scenario 21: A credit card the shared data does not have is reported by three people and verified

A small bank, **Gabe's Credit Union**, issues a card, **Gabe's Rewards Visa**, that is not in the shared data. Three different people each add it as their own, say it carries an extended warranty (+12 months on top of the maker's, up to 48 months in all), and buy something with it. Once three have, an operator verifies **the issuer**, then **the card**, then **its benefit**. Then the question is what happens to the three people's own cards, and to the next person who has the card.

Needs **three people (three accounts)** plus an **operator**, and a **fourth person** at the end. `python run_shared.py 21` plays all of them on new accounts and prints `PASS` or `FAIL` for each check below.

## Steps and what should happen (S6.1.4)

### Part 1: three people add the same new card

1. **Person 1** goes to **Cards** (`/cards`) and adds a card:
   - Issuer: not in the list, so **adds "Gabe's Credit Union" as a new issuer on their own list**.
   - Card: "Gabe's Rewards Visa", last four **2468**.
   - They say what the card carries: **extended warranty, +12 months, up to 48 months**.
2. Person 1 brings in files 01 and 02. The receipt's VISA 2468 matches their card. New Items shows the Makita saw with the Home Depot return window (90 days, ends **2027-01-19**), the Makita warranty, and the card's extended warranty from what they entered.
   - *Operator view:* one claim, **card issuer "Gabe's Credit Union", 1 account, not ready**. There is **no card or benefit claim yet**: they wait for a shared issuer.
3. **Persons 2 and 3** do the same on their own accounts: the issuer claim reaches **3 accounts, ready**.

### Part 2: the operator verifies it, in order

4. The operator **approves the issuer** (`POST /api/admin/claims/approve` with the claim's `id`). A new shared issuer "Gabe's Credit Union" exists. All three private issuers are replaced by it, and **each person's card now points at the shared issuer**.
5. **Two new claims appear, each already at 3 accounts and ready**: the **card** "Gabe's Rewards Visa" (the issuer's list does not have it), and the **benefit** "Extended warranty +12 months, up to 48 months in all" for that card.
6. The operator **approves the card**: it is added to the issuer's card list.
7. The operator looks the card's terms up and **approves the benefit** with the page they read: `{ "id": "<claim id>", "sourceUrl": "<the card's terms page>" }`. **Without the page it is refused.** The benefit becomes a shared, verified one for that card, with the operator's page as official-source evidence. If a benefit of that kind is already on file for the card, it also needs the date this one took effect (`effectiveFrom`) and becomes a new version.

### Part 3: what changes for the three people, and the next one

8. Each of the three people: their card shows **Gabe's Credit Union as a shared issuer** (not "yours"). The benefit they confirmed is still theirs and unchanged; nothing is overwritten.
9. A **fourth person** opens **Cards** and **picks "Gabe's Rewards Visa" from the list** (it is there now), with last four 2468, **without confirming any benefit**. They bring in files 01 and 02: the Makita saw has **the card's extended warranty ticked and added on its own from the published benefit**: it adds 12 months to the Makita warranty (12 months, within the 48-month limit), so coverage ends **2028-10-21**.

## Expected coverage for the fourth person (dates from the purchase date, 2026-10-21)

- Home Depot return window: 90 days, ends **2027-01-19**.
- Makita manufacturer warranty: 12 months, ends **2027-10-21**.
- Gabe's Rewards Visa extended warranty: +12 months, so coverage ends **2028-10-21** (verified, from the operator's page).

## Not covered here

- Other benefits of the card (purchase protection and so on): each is its own claim, made the same way.
- A card the shared data already has, with a different value for a benefit: that is listed as a possible change and needs `effectiveFrom` to approve.
- A screen for the operator: the operator steps are API calls (see the README).
