# S2.4.8 Read a warranty the receipt only states, and map it to a policy

**Feature:** F2.4 Photo and document reading  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** QA  
**Depends on:** S2.4.1 (document reader); S2.4.4 (checks); S3.1.5 (processing a New Item); S3.2.x (reference policies)  
**Pairs with:** S2.4.7 (a plan the person bought, which is its own priced line)  
**Related:** S2.7.2 (evidence behind a fact); S3.2.7 (unverified stores, brands and cards); S9.8.5 (what is worth tracking)  

As a **user**, I want a warranty that my receipt or invoice states ("2 YR MFR WARRANTY", "Road hazard warranty 50,000 mi", "heaters carry the manufacturer's warranty (6 years tank, 1 year parts)") to be noticed and attached to the item it belongs to, so I do not lose coverage the paper told me about, and I am never told something is covered when only a printed phrase says so.

## Why
Many receipts state a warranty without selling one. Nothing is bought; the paper just says it exists. Today the reader has no field for it, and the coverage step (S3.1.5) looks only at shared reference data (the store, the maker, the category, the dates, the model number and the card). So the statement is dropped: if the maker or store already has a policy in our data it is proposed whatever the receipt says, and if not the item shows no coverage at all. S2.4.7 handles the other case, a plan the person paid for as its own line; this story is the unpriced one.

Return windows ("returns within 90 days", "15 day return window") are not warranties and stay out of this story; they belong to the store's return policy.

## The model (one rule)
- A **stated warranty** is words on the document, kept as printed, with what can be read from them: the term (months, years or miles), the parts it names (compressor, parts, tank, glass), and **who stands behind it as the document says**: the maker ("MFR", "manufacturer's"), the seller, or unsaid. It is a clue, never a policy.
- It belongs to **one line**: the product it sits under or the one it names. A footer on a one-product receipt belongs to that product. Terms printed for a kind of product (an invoice saying "circulators 2 years, thermostats 3 years") belong to each line of that kind.
- It is **never turned into coverage on its own**. It either corroborates a policy we already know, or becomes a low-confidence "stated on receipt" coverage the person confirms.

## Acceptance criteria
- **The reader finds statements.** Each product or repair line gains an optional `stated_warranty`: the text as printed, the term when it is stated (a number of months or years, or a mileage), the provider as stated (maker, seller or not said), and any condition printed with it (register within 30 days, open-box items only). Nothing is guessed: a term the document does not state is left empty. The reader prompt is versioned and cached readings are not reused for this.
- **Not a plan, not a return window.** A priced plan line stays a plan (S2.4.7). A return or exchange window is not read here. A vague "may carry a manufacturer warranty, see the product page" states no term and is not kept as a warranty.
- **A mileage term is a term.** "50,000 mi" has no end date. It is shown as stated and never converted to a date.
- **Stored with the pending item**, in its own row per statement, with the reader's confidence; it goes away with the item and follows its line when a receipt of several things becomes several items.
- **Matched against what we know.** If a reference policy for the seller or the maker covers the same thing (the same kind of product, the same provider as the statement says, a term that agrees), the statement is shown as corroborating it ("the receipt says 2 years; the maker's policy says 2 years") and the policy's own confidence applies. A term that disagrees with the policy is shown as a difference, never silently overwritten (S2.7.14).
- **Otherwise an account-private coverage**, labelled "Stated on receipt, provider unconfirmed" when the provider is unclear, with low confidence. It appears as "may apply", is **not** attached by a quick add (which only takes coverage at 80% or more), and can be confirmed, edited or removed before adding.
- **Who gives it is asked, not assumed.** "Road hazard" is usually the shop's program and a mileage figure is usually the maker's tread-life warranty; a phrase that mixes both is recorded as provider unconfirmed, and the person (or a later policy match) settles it.
- **New Items shows it:** the row says what was found ("Receipt says: Road hazard warranty 50,000 mi"), whether it matched a known policy, and offers add, edit or remove.
- **Deterministic and unit tested** with plain readings (fake provider): matching against policies, the term arithmetic, mileage terms, a statement that disagrees with a policy, and a receipt with several lines each with its own statement.

## Not in this story
- A protection plan sold on the receipt (S2.4.7, S3.4.3).
- Return and exchange windows.
- Reading the policy from the warranty document itself (S3.2.x, S2.4.2).
- Adding makers or sellers to the shared data; an unlisted provider stays private to the account until it is reviewed (S3.2.7).

## Answer key and sample cases
The sample answer key has a per-line column `stated_warranty` (text as printed). It is not in `truth.json` yet: the eval scores only fields the reader returns, so it is added there when the reader field exists. The API returns nothing for it today, so TEST THIS shows no comparison for it.

Sample receipts with a stated warranty (`SampleData/receipts/pdf`): tires with a "Road hazard warranty 50,000 mi" sub-line (06); a repair invoice "Repair warranty: 90 days parts and labor" (13); footers "2 YR MFR WARRANTY" and similar on a generator (17, 36, 37, 39, 40) and a Honda one (38); "5 YR COMPRESSOR / 1 YR PARTS" and similar on mini-splits (20, 51 to 54); "10 YR LIMITED WARRANTY" on a stove (55); open-box items "carry the manufacturer warranty" (35); invoice terms for a kind of product (24, 71, 72, 73, 74, 75).

## Open questions
- **Per statement or per line.** A footer on a two-item receipt (a mini-split and a bracket kit) is assumed to belong to the unit it names, not to both.
- **How a statement is matched to a policy of the same kind.** Proposed: same provider as stated, same category, term within a month. Tune on the labelled set.
- **What a statement that names no provider becomes** when the seller and the maker both have policies. Proposed: shown against both, with neither chosen.
