# S2.4.9 Read a return or refund receipt, with negative amounts

**Feature:** F2.4 Photo and document reading  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M8 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S2.4.1 (document reader); S2.4.2 (one intake); S2.4.4 (checks)  
**Pairs with:** S5.4.20 (offer to mark the item refunded); S5.4.11 (record money recovered)  
**Related:** S2.4.7 (a plan that is refunded with its item); S2.2.4 (de-duplicate forwarded receipts)  

As a **user** who took something back, I want the return receipt, or the store's refund email, to be read as what it is, so the amounts, the refund method and the purchase it undoes are kept, and it is never mistaken for a new purchase.

## Why
A return receipt looks like a receipt with minus signs: "RETURN: WALL MOUNT TILT -39.99, TAX -2.20, TOTAL -42.19, AMEX REFUND ****7711". Reading it today fails in two ways. First, the reader is told nothing about returns, and the document classes have no "return". Second, negative amounts are thrown away: `ReceiptProducts.Money` and the document-facts parser accept only amounts of zero or more, so the total and the line price come back "nothing read" (seen on sample receipt 08). Refund confirmation emails and PDFs ("Your refund of $84.39 has been issued") have the same shape with different words. They are a normal part of owning things and a source of a "money recovered" fact (S5.4.11).

## The model (one rule)
- A **return document** is a document that undoes all or part of an earlier purchase. It has its own class, `return`, beside order confirmation, receipt and the rest (S2.4.2).
- Its amounts are **signed**: a refunded line, a refunded plan and the refund total are negative; a restocking fee or a price difference on an exchange is positive. A negative amount is a valid reading on a return document.
- It is **about an earlier purchase**. It prints a way back to it when it can (the original transaction or order number, the original date, the store), and the reader keeps what is printed.
- It **never becomes a new item** to track, and nothing in it is added to My Items.

## Acceptance criteria
- **A new class.** The reader answers `return` for a return receipt, a refund confirmation or an exchange slip. The prompt is versioned and old cached readings are not reused for this.
- **Negative amounts are read.** The total, the tax, each line's price and the refund total may be negative on a return document. A negative amount on any other document class is still treated as a likely misread (S2.4.4).
- **What is kept:** the store; the return date and number; the refund total; **how it was refunded** (the original card with its last four, store credit, a gift card, an exchange, cash, not said); each returned line with its brand, name, model number and negative price; refunded plan lines (S2.4.7); positive fee lines (restocking fee, price difference); and the **reference to the original** exactly as printed (transaction or order number, original date).
- **Checks:** the lines plus tax equal the total, with the signs; a refund larger than what could have been paid is flagged; a missing original reference is a normal case, not a problem.
- **Refund emails and PDFs** read the same way: "Refund issued", "Return received" and "Your refund" wording, an order number in the body, the refund method, the item and amount lines. No product is invented from a marketing block.
- **Exchange slips:** a returned line and a new line on one document; the net amount is the total; the new line is not added as a new item here (it will arrive with its own receipt).
- **Where it shows:** New Items lists it as a return, not as an item to add, and links to S5.4.20 (offer to mark the item refunded). It never appears as something to track.
- **Gift receipts and prices-hidden receipts** keep working: no prices is not a return (S2.4.10).
- **Tests:** deterministic with plain readings (fake provider): negative amounts on a return, the same amounts rejected on a normal receipt, the checks with signs, an exchange, a plan refund, a refund to store credit, and a refund email.

## Not in this story
- Finding the item the return belongs to and marking it refunded (S5.4.20).
- Recording the recovery (S5.4.11).
- Any change to how a normal receipt is read.

## Sample cases
`Testing/SampleData/receipts/pdf`: 08 (a thermal return to a card), and the return and refund documents 94 to 103 (a Home Depot, Best Buy, Target, Staples, Apple and Lowe's return, an exchange, and refund confirmation emails for Amazon, Best Buy and Home Depot). Most name the original purchase by a transaction or order number found on another sample receipt, so a match can be tested end to end.

## Status
Partly built 2026-10-20 (reader prompt document-v13).

**Built:** the `return` class (a document that says Return or Refund); negative amounts read on a return and thrown away on any other class (a negative on a normal document is flagged as a likely misread); the checks compare a refund by size; the reader keeps the refund method, the original order number and the original date as printed; each returned line is its own New Item of class return, with its negative price, and the refund total is kept as the receipt total; a return is never counted as something to track and cannot be accepted as a new item. Checked on samples 96, 99, 101 (signed price and total, method, original reference).

**Not built:** refund emails read as a mail (only their photos or PDFs work), exchange slips, a refunded protection plan on its own, the check that a refund is not larger than what could have been paid, and a line-by-line sum check with signs.

**History:** written 2026-10-19; before this the reader had no `return` class and dropped negative amounts, so a return receipt read as a store, a date and a card with no amounts.
