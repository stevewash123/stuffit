# S9.1.5 Native app wrapper for iPhone and Android

**Feature:** F9.1 App shell  
**Phase:** MVP  
**Milestone:** M5  
**Depends on:** S9.1.4 (Installable on a phone (PWA)); S2.2.6 (A receipt upload becomes a pending item)  

As a **user**, I want WarrantyCat in the app store on my phone, so I can share a receipt photo or screenshot to it from any app, and get reminders that arrive reliably.

## Why this matters
An installed web app cannot be a share target on iPhone, and web push on iOS is limited. Sharing a photo to the app is the easiest capture there is (S2.2.11), so for iPhone users the wrapper is the product, not a polish item. Decision (2026-10-03): build it after the proof of concept and before going live. It is not left as a "later option".

## Acceptance criteria
- The existing Angular app is wrapped with Capacitor and built for iOS and Android. One web codebase; the wrapper adds only what the browser cannot do.
- **Share target:** WarrantyCat appears in the share sheet for images and PDFs on both platforms. A shared file opens the "What is this for?" screen from S2.2.11: attach it to an existing item, or make a new pending item through the S2.2.6 upload path.
- **Camera:** the scan button uses the native camera, with the multi-page flow from S2.2.11.
- **Push:** reliable push notifications through the platform services, as the delivery channel F4.3 decides on. This story supplies the device token; F4.3 decides what is sent.
- **Wi-Fi only:** reports whether the phone is on Wi-Fi or mobile data, so the "Send photos only on Wi-Fi" setting (S2.2.11) works on iPhone as well as Android.
- **Sign-in and session** work in the wrapper, including after the phone restarts.
- **Store requirements:** developer accounts, privacy labels that match the privacy policy, app icons and screenshots, and a review pass on both stores. Allow time for store review before the live date.
- **Browser app unchanged:** the PWA keeps working for anyone who skips the store, and desktop is untouched.

## Not in this story
- Barcode scan of a model or serial number. It becomes easy once the wrapper exists; write it as its own story when wanted.
- Turning off the web push path if native push proves enough: decided under F4.3.

## Open questions
- Apple's review and its fee, and whether a billing screen inside the app has to use in-app purchase rather than the web checkout (S1.2). Check this early, because it can change the billing design.
- Whether to build Android first (the PWA share target already works there) to learn the wrapper cheaply, then iPhone.
