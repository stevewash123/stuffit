# S9.8.5 Choose what is worth tracking

**Feature:** F9.8 Settings & user configuration  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M8 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S9.8.1 (Settings page); S9.8.3 (Region and display preferences, for the currency); S9.9.1 (New Items list); S2.2.5 (Pending items and their API)  
**Related:** S2.2.7 (suggest skipping receipts that do not need tracking); S2.2.12 (leave out services and subscriptions); S2.4.7 (protection plans on a receipt); S9.9.4 (Dismiss: do not track); S2.4.5 (keep what the person corrected)  

As a **user**, I want to say how small a purchase is still worth tracking, so New Items leads with the things I would actually claim on and does not make me tick off every cable and filter.

## Why
A receipt lists every line: the refrigerator, its plan, and a $15 hose. Most people do not want the hose, a few do, and nobody agrees on where the line is. It is a taste, not a fact, so the person should set it. It also cannot be a hard rule. A $40 smoke detector or kettle can have a warranty, a $30 part can be the one that fails, and a wrongly hidden item can cost someone a claim, while a wrongly listed one costs a tick. So the setting changes what is suggested and what is pre-ticked. It never removes a line.

## The model (one rule)
- **One number, with a default.** "Track things that cost at least $50." The default is $50 in the account's currency (S9.8.3). The person can change it, or choose "track everything" (zero) or "only big purchases" (a high number).
- **A threshold is one signal among several.** A line is marked "probably not worth tracking" when it is under the number **and** at least one soft signal agrees. Price alone is not enough to demote a durable, named product.
- **Soft signals** (each is a clue, none is a verdict):
  - under the threshold and not the main item on the receipt;
  - a word that usually means a wear, install or accessory part (filter, gasket, belt, hose, bracket, cable, adapter, install kit);
  - a used-up part (ink, bulbs, cartridges, filters);
  - bought alongside a much larger item;
  - the line is labour for installing or repairing something, under the threshold.
- **Always tracked, whatever it costs:** the main item on a receipt; any line a protection plan covers (a plan shows the person thought it worth protecting); and anything the person has ticked before in the same way (see learning, below).
- **Effects, all soft:** the line is sorted below the rest and collapsed under "Smaller things (N)"; it is not pre-ticked on a quick add; its row says why ("Under $50 · filter"). One click ticks it. Nothing is hidden, dismissed or deleted.

## Acceptance criteria
- **A setting on the Settings page** (S9.8.1): "Track things worth at least [ $50 ]", with a short explanation and the choices "Everything" and "Only larger purchases". Stored as a typed column beside the other preferences (S9.8.3), not in a key-value bag; changes are written to the audit log with old and new values.
- **A hint on every pending item line**, worked out on the server by plain rules (no model call): `likely`, `unsure` or `unlikely` worth tracking, with the reason text. Deterministic and unit tested with plain receipts; no live service.
- **New Items** (S9.9.1) groups `unlikely` lines under "Smaller things", collapsed, below the others, each with its reason and a one-click "Track this". `unsure` lines stay in the main list with the reason shown. Quick add (S9.9.3) ticks `likely` lines only.
- **Two values.** The number is compared with the line total for what is at stake (a return takes back the whole line, so 24 studs at $110.16 are worth recovering), and the unit price decides only whether a warranty is plausible ("WATER FILTER x2, $69.98" has a warranty question judged at $34.99 each). A consumable at or over the number is a return-only item, not an item to be asked about a warranty (S9.2.11). A line with no price is never demoted on price, and counts as at or over the number for colour.
- **Plans keep their item.** A line with a protection plan attached (S2.4.7) is `likely` whatever it costs, and the plan is never offered on its own.
- **Changing the number re-sorts** what is waiting in New Items, never what was already accepted. Accepted items and dismissed ones are untouched.
- **Learns, gently.** When the person ticks lines the hint called `unlikely` (for example three filters over a few weeks), a small note offers "You often track things under $50. Lower the amount to $20?" with Accept and Not now. It never changes the setting on its own. Dismissing lines that the hint called `likely` offers the opposite. Corrections are kept (S2.4.5).
- **Reading stays separate.** The reader reports what a line is (product, plan, consumable, fee, repair); the threshold decides what to suggest. A reader that disagrees with the hint is not wrong, and a change to the setting never needs a reread.
- **Per person.** The setting belongs to the person (S9.8.3). In a shared account each person sees their own sorting; what is accepted belongs to the account as before (F1.3).
- **Explainable.** Anywhere a hint appears the reason can be read in plain words, with a link to the setting.

## Not in this story
- Rules by store, brand or product category ("always track tools"). S2.2.7 holds the per-store rule; per-category rules can follow once this setting has real use.
- Leaving a whole receipt out (groceries, subscriptions): S2.2.7 and S2.2.12.
- Changing what the reader extracts, or its prompt (F2.4).

## Notes
- **Same rule for the labelled set.** The sample answer key (`Testing/SampleData/receipts/pdf-as-jpg/answer-key-by-item.csv`) already has a `certainty` column (`sure` or `judgment`) set by the same signals at the $50 default. When this lands, the reading measure (S2.4.3) scores `sure` lines strictly and reports `judgment` lines separately, so the app is not marked wrong for a call that is a matter of taste. The two should share one definition of the signals, not two copies.
- **Why not lean on price alone.** In the sample set, price alone would flag a $24.99 toaster and a $44.90 skillet as small, and miss a $39.99 wall mount, which is an accessory. The signals together did better; the number only breaks ties.
- **Decided (Steve, 2026-10-19): per person.** The minimum is each person's own setting, and recipients' reminders use their own. A new household member starts from the account owner's value, copied when they join (decided 2026-10-19, S9.2.11). Single user is the focus; multi-user is left room for and not tested in depth.
- **Open question: what "alongside a larger item" means.** Proposed: the line costs less than a fifth of the receipt's largest line. Tune on the labelled set, not by feel.
