# S8.1.2 The owner invites employees by email

**Feature:** F8.1  **Phase:** v2  
**Depends on:** S8.1.1; S1.3.4 (invite a person, built here for business accounts); S1.3.5 (remove a person or leave); S7.6.4 (transactional email)

As the **owner**, I want to invite my employees by email, so they can add and see the business's things under their own login.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Only the owner invites** (decided, Steve 2026-11-06). Employees cannot invite anyone.
- **Invite by email**: the owner enters an address; WarrantyCat **sends an invitation email** from the business ("<Business name> invited you to WarrantyCat") with a single-use, unguessable link that expires (7 days, configuration). The owner sees who is invited and can **resend** or **revoke** an invite before it is accepted.
- **Following the link proves the address**, so the person is not asked to verify it again (S1.1.1). They sign up or sign in and join as an **employee**. They keep **their own login, notification preferences and time zone**; the owner never sees their password.
- **A person who already has a WarrantyCat login** opens the invite link, signs in with it (which proves the address) and accepts: the business is added to their memberships and their personal account stays (S8.1.6, which replaces the earlier MVP fallback of refusing such addresses). A new address signs up and gets the business membership and an empty personal account.
- **Limit during the free year: owner plus two employees** (S8.1.5). Inviting a fourth person is refused with a plain message ("Your business account includes you and two employees"); nothing else is blocked.
- **The People page** (Settings): every person with role, status (invited, active) and joined date; owner actions: resend, revoke, **remove an employee**. An employee can **leave** the business (with a confirm); they lose that membership and its items and keep their login and their personal account (S8.1.6).
- **What happens to an employee's items** when they are removed or leave: items stay with the business (they were the business's); the item's "entered by" keeps their name as history; reminders for items they followed go to the owner.
- **Email addresses** are used for the invite only; no marketing; the invite says plainly who invited them and for what.
- The owner is told (a notification) when an invite is accepted.

## Notes
Do not let an invited address see any business item before accepting. Rate-limit invites per account per day (configuration).

## Status
Built (branch feat-business, not merged). Owner invites by email (POST /api/business/people/invite): single-use link (random token, only its SHA-256 stored), expires in 7 days (Business:InviteDays), resend (new link, old one dies), revoke, daily limit per account (Business:InvitesPerDay, default 20, resends count). The invitation email is a transactional email sent like the sign-up link (not through the preference grid), subject "<Business> invited you to WarrantyCat". The link opens /join: a new address sets a password and agrees to the terms (no second verification), joins as employee and gets an empty personal account of its own; an address that already has a login signs in and accepts, and the business is added to its memberships (S8.1.6). The owner gets a business_person_joined notification (business accounts only in the preference grid). People page in Settings (role, invited/active, joined, resend/revoke/remove/make owner; employee sees names only and can Leave with a confirm). Removing or leaving drops only that membership: items stay, what they owned goes to the owner, what they followed is followed by the owner, entered-by keeps their name; their token for the business stops working at once. An invited address sees nothing before accepting (no session is issued by an invitation).
Not built: a notification-grid entry for the invitee (they are not a user yet, by design).
