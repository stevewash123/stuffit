# S3.1.8 Lifetime warranties: whose lifetime, and can it be passed on

**Feature:** F3.1 Coverage tracking
**Phase:** v2
**Depends on:** S3.1.1 (Add or edit a coverage); the warranty reader (S2.4.1)

As a **user**, I want a "lifetime" warranty shown as what it really promises, so I know whether it still helps if I give the item away, sell it or pass it to my family.

## Why
"Lifetime" has no single meaning. It can be the life of the product, the life of the product line (ends when the maker stops making the model), or the life of the original purchaser. In every case the company decides in practice what counts as the end of the lifetime. Most "limited lifetime" warranties are also original purchaser only and not transferable, so an heir or a buyer may have no claim except the maker's goodwill.

## Acceptance criteria
- A coverage can be marked lifetime, with no end date (not zero, not a guessed number of years)
- It records whose lifetime: product, product line, or original purchaser; or "not stated"
- It records transferability: transferable, not transferable (original purchaser only), transferable once or for a fee, or not stated
- The item page says it in plain words, for example "Lifetime of the product. Original purchaser only. Not transferable. The maker decides when it has worn out."
- When an item is marked given away or sold, a lifetime coverage that is not transferable says it ends with the owner
- The warranty reader fills these from the document and leaves "not stated" rather than guessing; the person can correct them
- Lifetime is not "never expires". A lifetime coverage can become expired, and the app treats that as normal: it has no end date, but it can be ended with a reason (given away or sold, the maker went out of business or dropped the model, the claim was refused as worn out, the owner died). Once ended it shows as expired like any other coverage
- A lifetime coverage shows as "Lifetime, can end" rather than a permanent green, and the item page lists what could end it
- No end-date reminder can fire, so a yearly check-in asks "still covered? still have the receipt?" and the answer can end it

## Help text (tooltip or side panel, wherever warranties are explained)
For a lifetime warranty, explain it plainly and briefly:
- Usually means the normal use life of the product; normal wear and depreciation are not covered
- Sometimes refers to the product line, so it can end when the maker stops making the model
- Can be denied at the company's discretion
- Might not be transferable to new owners

## Notes
- The point for us is the reminder that a policy can become expired even when the document says lifetime.
