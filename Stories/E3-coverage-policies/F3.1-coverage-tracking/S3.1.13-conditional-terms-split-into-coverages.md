# S3.1.13 A warranty with a condition is split into a base coverage and an optional one

**Feature:** F3.1 Coverage tracking
**Phase:** v2 (built)
**Depends on:** S3.1.1 (coverage); S3.1.2 (proposals); S3.1.11 (a start that needs the owner's date); the warranty reader (S2.4.1); the to-do and reminder machinery (E4)
**Idea from:** Steve, 2026-11-05

As a **user**, when a warranty gives a short term and a longer one if I do something (register, send in a card, have it serviced), I want both shown as separate coverages: the short one active, the longer one waiting until I say I did it, so I am never told I have three years when I only have 90 days.

## Why
Some warranties are "90 days, or 3 years if you register within 30 days". One coverage cannot say that honestly: showing 3 years claims coverage the person may not have; showing 90 days hides the reason to act. Two coverages can: the base one is real now, and the optional one is a coverage the person can earn, with a deadline that becomes a to-do. Sample: `Testing/SampleData/warranties/edge-registration-required.pdf` (Trailblaze TB-350: register in 30 days to extend 90 days to 3 years).

## The principle
When a warranty has conditions or stages, split it into separate coverages instead of one clever one. This is the general rule for hard shapes: full term plus a longer limited term, free replacement then prorated, parts and labor on different clocks. This story is the first where the second coverage is optional.

## Acceptance criteria
- **Two proposals from one document.** When the document states a base term and a longer term that depends on a stated condition, the reader proposes both: the base coverage, selected by default, and the conditional coverage, not selected, labelled with the condition ("3 years if you register within 30 days")
- **Never invented.** If the document gives a longer term without saying what it depends on, or says a condition without a length, nothing is split. It reads as it does today ("no clear length") and the person adds the coverage themselves. The condition's wording is shown as quoted from the document
- **The conditional coverage is not active until the person says so.** It is listed on the item page as "3 years if you register by <date>. Not counted yet." It does not appear in the days-left figure, the item's attention level, Home counts, reminders for its end date, the yearly-cost figure or claim guidance as covered. The base coverage does all of that on its own
- **The deadline is real.** When the condition has a time limit that can be worked out (30 days from the purchase date, or from the start date once the person enters it, S3.1.11), the item gets a to-do "Register {item} by <date> to extend the warranty to 3 years", due a few days before the deadline, with the usual reminder. If the start date is still to be confirmed, the deadline is shown as "30 days after the start date" and the to-do waits for the date
- **"I did it."** On the item page and in the to-do, one action: **I registered**. It asks for the date (defaults to today), marks the optional coverage active from that date's rules, finishes the to-do and shows the longer term. It can be undone within the session
- **The two never double up.** Once the optional coverage is active it **replaces** the base coverage for counts and reminders: the base one stays in the list, marked "replaced by the {name} coverage", with its history. Undoing "I registered" brings the base back
- **The deadline passes.** After the deadline with no "I registered" the optional coverage reads "Not earned: the registration window ended <date>". It is kept (the person may have registered and forgotten to say) and **I registered** still works, but asks the person to confirm they registered **in time**, with the date. The to-do is closed as missed, not deleted
- **Not selected means not added.** If the person leaves the optional coverage unticked when accepting the suggestion, only the base coverage is created and no to-do is made; it can be added later from the item page, with its condition and deadline
- **Whose condition.** The condition is about the owner doing something; a condition that only the maker or the seller can meet (a warranty that extends if the dealer installs it) is shown in the terms and not split
- **Other conditions.** The same screens serve any owner action with a deadline: send in a registration card, register online, have the first service done by a dealer. The reader recognises registration first; other conditions are added one at a time and never guessed
- **Plain words, not advice.** The page says what the document says and that the maker decides in practice. "Not counted yet" is a statement about WarrantyCat's counting, not about the maker's willingness
- The conditional coverage, its condition text, its deadline and whether the person confirmed it are in the data export
- Shared policies (verified, community) that have the same two-stage shape use the same two coverages for the item. Where a shared policy gives only the longer term with a registration rule, the item gets the same split, not the long term alone

## Notes
- The registration-dependent start (a term that starts at registration, S3.1.11) is a different thing and stays as it is: there the one coverage starts later. Here there are two coverages and the second one is earned.
- Data: the optional coverage carries a **condition** (text and kind), a **condition deadline**, and a **condition met on** date; "active" means condition met. A to-do kind for it reuses the start-date to-do pattern.
- Scenario: extend Scenario 54 Part B (registration-dependent term) with the split, using `edge-registration-required.pdf`, and add steps for the deadline passing (move the clock) and for undo.

## Status
Built 2026-11-05 (API migration `ConditionalCoverage`, UI; reader prompt `item-warranty-v5`). All three doors (item page, Add a purchase, New Items) split; quick add never attaches the optional coverage. Not built: shared policies with a registration rule, and adding an optional coverage by hand (only by re-reading the document). Decisions: the optional coverage starts at the base start and runs its full length; the deadline counts from the base start; a missed to-do is closed as dismissed with a note; "90 days" reads as 3 months.
