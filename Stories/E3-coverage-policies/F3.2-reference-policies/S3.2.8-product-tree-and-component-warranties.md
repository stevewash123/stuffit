# S3.2.8 A product tree, and warranties made of components

**Feature:** F3.2 Reference policies  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M6 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S3.2.3 (starter reference data); S3.2.5 (policy evidence and confidence); S2.4.2 (one intake for every document)  

As a **user**, I want my item matched to the right brand, line, size and model number, so WarrantyCat shows the warranty that really applies to it, with each part of that warranty (the whole product, the ice maker, the compressor) and its own end date.

## What real warranties look like (found 2026-10-06 in Samsung's own document)
Samsung's Bespoke refrigerator warranty (U.S., 2022) is one document with four terms: 1 year parts and labor on the refrigerator; 2 years on the ice maker; 5 years parts and labor on the sealed system; 10 years parts and 5 years labor on the digital inverter compressor. An addendum extends the whole refrigerator to 2 years, but only for about 60 listed model numbers bought between October 1, 2022 and September 30, 2023. So a warranty is a set of component terms, split into parts and labor, sometimes limited by model numbers and purchase dates. Today a policy is one term per brand and category plus free text.

## The structure (decided with Steve, 2026-10-06)
- **A tree of any depth under each brand, with free level names.** Companies differ: some keep brands completely separate (a company owns several brands, marketed apart); others are one brand with a few lines on one sales page (Penn: Battle and Fierce), with sizes (1500, 2500, 3500...) beneath, or with each size as its own model (FIERCE2500). Capture the hierarchy as it is; the names of the levels are free text, only there to help a person recognise the right node. Nothing in the logic depends on a level's name.
- **Company above brand.** A parent-company link on a party (Maytag and KitchenAid under Whirlpool). A policy can sit at company level.
- **Node:** name, optional level label, aliases ("Fierce III", "FIERCE3"), category, and its place in the tree. The existing model catalog (brand, series, model) is the two-level case of this and moves into the tree.
- **Model and serial numbers belong on the leaf.** A leaf carries every exact model number it goes by (regional and colour suffixes such as RF23A967535/AA and /AC), compared after normalising case, spaces and dashes; it may also carry a prefix pattern for families whose numbers encode line, colour and region (RF23A9675*). An exact model-number match beats any matching by words and goes straight to that leaf. A model number that contradicts the brand named is put to the person, not trusted.
- **Serial numbers** are kept per item. They identify the unit (duplicates, claims need them) and, only where a brand publishes how its serials encode a manufacture date or range, can limit which policy applies. Decoding is a later addition, brand by brand.
- **A policy attaches to any node (or company).** It has terms: component, parts period, labor period. Starts from purchase or registration as today. Eligibility can limit it: purchase-date window, model numbers.
- **Which terms apply to an item:** walk up from its deepest matched node; the nearest policy wins for each component, and components it does not list are inherited from above. (Samsung's addendum then reads as written: "2 years on the refrigerator for these models and dates, all other terms unchanged.")

## Matching an item to a node
By exact model number first; otherwise by brand, then by the words and numbers in the item's name, as deep as the match is confident ("Penn Fierce III 2500" reaches the 2500 node; "Penn Fierce" stops at Fierce and offers "which size?"). Only a brand known: the brand's own policy. The document reader's brand, model name, model number and serial feed the same matcher. A model number a person confirms against a node, for a node that lacks it, is remembered as a suggested alias (promoted after independent agreement, as policies are).

## On the item
One warranty line for the whole product plus one line per component, each with its own end date and reminder. Where parts and labor differ the line says so ("parts to 2036, labor to 2031").

## Verification
A brand's own warranty document is the evidence: read once, recorded with its URL and version date as an official-source check (S3.2.5). An AI search summary is a lead, never evidence.

## Phasing
1. Tree, policy terms and lookup; the Samsung refrigerator policy entered from the primary document as the pilot; components on the item page.
2. Penn (Battle and Fierce, sizes) from Penn's own documents, as the test of the tree.
3. Admin editor; a document kind so the reader can pull terms out of a warranty PDF; serial decoding where a brand publishes it.

## Open decisions
- Add the parent-company link on parties? (proposed: yes)
- Nearest wins per component with the rest inherited, or the nearest policy replaces everything above it? (proposed: per component)
- Coverage lines: one per component, reusing the coverage screens and reminders (proposed), or one warranty line that expands.

## Placeholders
The fake placeholder policies (`--seed-placeholders`) stay one simple term per brand and category until this exists.

## Status
Phase 1 built 2026-10-07 (API and UI, tested, not browser-checked):
- Data model (migrations ProductTreeAndWarrantyComponents, PolicyScopeOverlapRule; dev and test): `Party.ParentPartyId`; `ProductNode` tree (name, level label, aliases, model numbers, prefixes); `ManufacturerPolicy.NodeId`, purchase window (`PurchasedFrom/To`), listed `ModelNumbers`; `ManufacturerPolicyTerm` (component, parts months, labor months); `Coverage.Component` and `LaborEndDate`. The database's no-overlap rule for maker policies now separates by node, "listed models or not", and purchase window.
- Matching: `ModelNumbers.Normalize` and `ProductNodeMatcher` (exact number, longest prefix, then words, down the tree; a size alone never picks a node).
- Lookup: `PolicyLookupService.ManufacturerWarrantyAsync`: the brand's general policy (restricted policies are not general), the most specific applicable policy for the whole-product term, each component from the nearest policy, falling back to the parent company. Proposals carry `components`; accepting the maker's warranty (or a quick add) stores one coverage per component with its own end date and reminder (reminder text names the part).
- UI: the suggestion lists the parts; a component line on the item shows "Maker warranty: Compressor" and "Labor is covered until ...".
- Data: Samsung refrigerators, from Samsung's own warranty PDF (2022 Bespoke version): general terms (1 year; ice maker 2 years; sealed system 5 years parts and labor; digital inverter compressor 10 years parts and 5 years labor) and the 2-year addendum for 65 listed models bought 2022-10-01 to 2023-09-30, as official-source evidence. It lives in `reference-data/samsung.json`, imported with `--import-reference` (see HANDOFF).
Not built yet (phase 2 and 3): Penn Battle and Fierce as the real test of the tree; seeding the tree for existing brands (the model catalog still has its own two levels); an admin editor for nodes, terms and eligibility; a document kind so the reader can pull terms out of a warranty PDF; serial decoding; the item remembering its matched node (it is matched again each time).
