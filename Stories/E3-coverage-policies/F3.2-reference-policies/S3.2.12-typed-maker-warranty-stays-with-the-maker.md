# S3.2.12 A maker's warranty you type in stays with the maker

**Feature:** F3.2 Reference data & versioned policies  
**Phase:** v2  
**Depends on:** S3.2.1 (party and versioned policy model); S3.2.5 (policy source and confidence); S3.1.2 (coverage proposal); S3.1.5 (New Items); S3.5.4 (vehicle coverage start)  
**Related:** S3.1.7 (item page view and edit); S3.2.9 (shared policies grow); the "Your own entry may be out of date" note (SupersededRules)  
**Decided by:** Steve, 2026-11-06.

As a **user** who types in the maker's warranty on an item by hand, I want WarrantyCat to remember it for that maker, so the next thing I buy from them, or a New Item waiting from them, is offered the same warranty instead of making me type it again.

## Acceptance criteria
- **The warranty stays with the maker.** A maker's warranty typed in by hand on an item (a coverage whose source is You) is saved as the account's own manufacturer policy (OwnerAccountId = the account) for the item's maker and category, with the term in months, the start rule (the purchase date) and the terms text as typed. It is a policy like any other the account owns: it is found by the same lookup, offered in the same "could apply" list with its checkbox, and treated no differently from one added from a receipt or a document, apart from its source.
- **Manual source, lower confidence.** It carries one evidence row of the new kind Manual (PolicyEvidence, EvidenceKind.Manual, once per account). Weight 0.15, below a typed policy (0.25): Unconfirmed, never Likely, never ticked on its own. Its reason reads "Your own entry, typed in by hand; not checked against the maker's source." It reuses the usual confidence path (PolicyConfidence, PolicyTrustService); there is no separate one.
- **Never applied by itself.** It is offered with its checkbox and added only when the person ticks it; a quick add of a New Item never attaches it.
- **Offered to later items and waiting New Items.** A later purchase or item, and a New Item, from the same maker and category is offered it. Saving it marks that account's waiting New Items to be checked again (the existing save hook), so they pick it up.
- **Not offered back to the item it came from.** The typed item already has the maker's warranty, so the usual "already taken" rule hides the offer there.
- **Private to the account.** Another account never sees it (the policy query filter).
- **No duplicates.** One open policy of the account per maker and category. The same term again reuses it. A different term typed later replaces the term of the account's Manual policy (the latest entry stands); the account's own policy from anywhere better (a document, or entered in settings) is never overwritten. A term the shared list already holds for that maker and category (same months) is not kept: the shared one is offered with more confidence.
- **Edits follow.** Changing the term or the terms text of the typed coverage updates the policy. An edit that leaves them alone (a phone number, a note) does not touch it, so editing an old item never undoes a newer entry.
- **Deleting keeps it.** Deleting the coverage or the item does not delete the policy: what was learnt about the maker stays. The person can retire it with the existing policy tools; a new entry after that starts the day after the retired one ends.
- **Not kept** for: a vehicle or a part of one (its coverage starts differently, S3.5.4); an item with no maker; an item with no category (a policy for any kind of product would be too broad to guess from one entry); a private sale (the term typed on a used item may be what was left, not what the maker gives); a lifetime, optional (conditional), component, certified, prorated or start-unknown coverage (not a plain term); a coverage typed for someone other than the item's maker; a term that cannot be told (no months, and dates that are not a whole number of months).
- **Superseded note unchanged.** "Your own entry may be out of date" (SupersededRules, CoverageSupersededDismissal) works as before.
- No company, plan or insurance setup is needed.

## Out of scope
- Vehicles (to come with S3.5.4 once their coverage start is settled).
- Sharing the typed warranty with other accounts (S3.2.9 and the community path decide that from documents, not from typing).
- A maker's registration or installation start rule from a typed coverage: the policy counts from the purchase date.

## Notes
- The Manual kind is only a new value of an enum stored by name: no migration.
- A private policy beats a shared one at the same category level in the lookup (existing rule). A typed term that differs from a Verified shared one is shown with the existing "differs from the verified" note; a Manual policy is low confidence, so it is offered unticked.
- The save hook for waiting items now treats evidence for a policy only one account owns (Manual, UserTyped) as that account's change, not everyone's.
