# S3.2.6 Purchase channel: online or in store

**Feature:** F3.2 Reference data & versioned policies  
**Phase:** MVP (field, labels, defaults); v2 (wizard question)  
**Milestone:** M6 (proposed); wizard question with the guidance work (S5.4.x)  
**Depends on:** S3.2.1; S3.1.1 (coverage proposal); S3.2.5 (confidence); S2.2.2 (extraction)  

As a **user**, I want to say whether I bought something online or in a store, and see which of a store's policies apply to that, so the return window and the way to return it are right for how I actually bought it.

## Why
Many stores differ by channel: the window may count from delivery (online) or from the sale (in store), the fee can differ, and where you may return it (by mail, in a store, either) can differ. Today the purchase has no channel and a seller policy has one window, with the online terms only in free text. A New Balance purchase made in a New Balance store would be counted from a delivery date it never had.

## Acceptance criteria

### Purchase channel
- A purchase has a channel: **Online**, **In store**, **Unknown**. It is optional in the forms and never blocks saving.
- It always has a value. The default is chosen by the best signal, in this order: what the source says; the store's own pattern (a store with only online policies); else Unknown.
- **An email is not evidence of an online purchase.** Stores email in-store customers a receipt too (Home Depot asks for an email at the register). Only the kind of email counts (S2.2.2 already classifies them):
  - Order confirmation, shipped or delivered notice: Online. These carry an order placed online, a shipping address, a tracking number or a delivery date.
  - E-receipt (a copy of a register receipt): not Online. In store when it shows store signals (a store number or address, register or cashier, a card tender line like "Visa ****1234", no shipping or delivery); otherwise Unknown. Pickup-in-store orders are an online order picked up in store; mark them Online with return method "in store".
  - A scanned or uploaded paper receipt with register signals: In store. A bare photo with none: Unknown.
  - Manual entry: Unknown until the user chooses.
- The channel carries its source (set by email, set by receipt, set by user) like any extracted fact (S2.7.2). A user's choice always wins and is never overwritten by a later email.
- Editable on the purchase form ("Where did you buy it?"), on New Items before accepting, and on the item page.
- Unknown is allowed to stay unknown. The app does not nag; it shows the options below instead.

### Policies by channel
- A seller policy can say which channel it applies to: Online, In store, or Both (the default; every existing policy stays Both). A store can have several policies for the same kind of coverage, one per channel.
- A policy also carries its **return method**: by mail, in store, or either, plus the "window counts from delivery" flag that already exists.
- The seed and operator forms get a channel selector; an unsplit store stays a single Both policy. A split is only entered when someone has read it on the source page, with the usual evidence record (S3.2.5).

### Choosing among applicable policies
- When a purchase gets suggested coverage (S3.1.1), every applicable policy is offered as an option, each labeled with its channel, for example "Return window · New Balance (online): 30 days from delivery, return by mail or at a store" and "Return window · New Balance (in store): 30 days from purchase, return to a store".
- If the purchase channel is known, the matching policy is selected by default and the others are listed under "Other ways this store sells" and not selected. If the channel is Unknown, the policies are all shown with their labels and none of the channel-specific ones is pre-selected, except where all of them agree (same window, same method), which is shown once.
- Picking one changes the purchase's channel to match when the user chose it by hand, and asks "Was this bought online?" if the choice contradicts a source-set channel.
- The confidence band and reason (S3.2.5) show per option, so an unconfirmed in-store window is marked as such.
- Accepting a suggestion saves the channel and return method in the coverage's own snapshot; later policy edits do not change it.

### Item page and wizard
- The item page shows the channel next to the purchase date ("Online") and the return method in the return window row ("Return by mail or at a store").
- v2: the return and claim wizard (S5.4.x) asks **How did you buy this?** (online, in store, not sure) near the start, when the channel is Unknown or the store has policies that differ by channel. The answer picks the policy, fixes the deadline and the return steps, and is saved back to the purchase. When the answer differs from the stored channel, it asks before changing it.
- Warranty coverage (manufacturer) does not depend on channel by default. A manufacturer policy may say it applies only to authorized sellers; then the channel and the seller are checked against the brand's authorized list and a mismatch is a warning, not a block.

## Notes
- Channel is not the same as how the user captured it (email, upload, manual): that is the purchase source and stays as it is.
- Unknown must stay a safe state. With Unknown and policies that differ, show the options; do not guess silently.
- Research cost is in the data: each split is a store's online and in-store terms read from its pages. Seed only what was read; mark the rest unconfirmed (S3.2.5).
- First data to split: New Balance (online 30 days from delivery; in-store terms to be read).
