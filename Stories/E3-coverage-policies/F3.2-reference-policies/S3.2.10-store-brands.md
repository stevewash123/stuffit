# S3.2.10 Store brands: a brand that belongs to a retailer

**Feature:** F3.2 Reference data & versioned policies  
**Phase:** v2  
**Depends on:** S3.2.1 (party and versioned policy model); S3.2.8 (product tree and component warranties); S5.4.2 (claim steps are reference data)  
**Related:** S5.4.1 (how-to card); S3.1.2 (ranked coverage)  
**Found by:** Steve, 2026-10-28: a shovel sold under a retailer's own name with a 3-year warranty. Is that a store warranty or a manufacturer warranty, when the store did not make it?

As a **user** holding something sold under a retailer's own name, I want its warranty shown as the brand's warranty and the claim guidance to say to take it back to that store, so I am not told to contact a manufacturer I have never heard of.

## Why this matters
A store brand (a private label) is made by a contract factory and sold under the retailer's name. The warranty is the brand owner's promise, which is the retailer's, and it is claimed at the store: by returning the item, often to any store in the chain or co-op, usually with proof of purchase. The maker behind it is usually not named, does not deal with customers, and is not who to call. Today the guidance for a brand warranty is written for a maker. For a store brand that sends the person to the wrong place.

The three things are different and stay different:
- **A return window** is the store's rule for anything it sells (S3.2.1 seller policy).
- **A brand warranty** belongs to a product line and says how long the brand's promise lasts (manufacturer policy).
- **A store brand** is a brand whose owner is a retailer. Its warranty is a brand warranty, and the retailer is also the place to claim it.

## Acceptance criteria
- **A brand can belong to a retailer.** A shared brand can name the store (or stores) it is a store brand of. It is data on the brand (a link to a company that has the store role), not a note on each product. A company that is both a store and a brand (the same name, both roles) is the common case; a store with several brands of its own (a tool line, a home line) links each.
- **Its warranty is recorded as the brand's warranty,** with the length, start rule and source as for any maker (S3.2.1, S3.2.5, and the operator's Brands page). Nothing about the warranty changes; only who it is claimed from.
- **Claim guidance follows the link.** For an item whose brand is a store brand, the "something broke" and repair or return guidance (S5.4.1, S5.4.14) says to take it back to the store, not to contact the maker, and uses the retailer's own claim steps (S5.4.2): where to return it (any store in the chain, the one it was bought at, online), what to bring (receipt, the item, the original packaging), and the retailer's help page or phone. The maker is not named.
- **Ranked coverage still compares them.** On the item, the brand warranty and the store's return window both show (S3.1.2); the return window usually ends first, and the brand warranty keeps going. The page says which is which in plain words ("the store's return window", "the brand's warranty").
- **Unknown who made it is fine.** No maker is asked for or stored for a store brand. A user entering a store-brand item picks the brand; the store is filled from the link when it matches the one they bought from, and asked when it does not (the same brand sold by another retailer, or the brand's owner is not the store on the receipt).
- **A brand sold in several retailers is not a store brand.** The link means "this retailer owns the brand". A maker's own brand sold in many stores stays a maker's brand with its own claim steps. A store that only resells a maker's brand has no link.
- **Operators set and check it.** On the shared brand page an operator links the brand to the retailer it belongs to, with the source page that says so (the retailer's own page, as in S3.2.5). A user who adds a brand that is clearly a store's own (the name matches the store they bought from) is offered the link as a suggestion that joins the review queue (S6.1.1); it is not applied to shared data until an operator confirms it.
- **Receipts and documents help.** A receipt or warranty document that names the retailer as the one that stands behind the product (for example "our warranty", "guaranteed by [the store]") is read as a suggestion that the brand is the store's own, shown with its quote for the user or operator to confirm, never applied silently.

## Out of scope
- Naming or tracking the contract maker of a store brand.
- A store's extended plans (S3.4.5): those are plans bought on top of a warranty, not the brand's own.

## Decided
- The claim steps for a store brand live on the store, with the brand's warranty terms on the brand, because the route is the store's (Steve, 2026-10-28).

## Open questions (deferred)
- Brand lines that change hands (a retailer sells its brand, or buys one) need effective dates on the link, as policies have (S3.2.1).
- Whether a retailer co-op (member stores that honor each other's returns) needs its own marker.
