# S3.3.7 Say what a debit or prepaid card does and does not cover

**Feature:** F3.3 Payment methods & card benefits  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** unassigned  
**Depends on:** S3.3.1 (Add a card); S3.3.2 (Confirm card benefits)  
**Needs a migration:** yes (a card type), so it waits for the desktop code to be merged.

As a **user** who pays with a debit or prepaid card, I want WarrantyCat to tell me plainly what that card does and does not add, so I am not surprised later and I know what a safer choice looks like.

## Why
Debit and prepaid cards usually carry no extended warranty or purchase protection, so WarrantyCat has nothing to add for a purchase made with one. Fraud protection on a debit card is generally weaker than on a credit card, because the money leaves the account before a dispute is settled. Many people use debit on purpose, to avoid debt or to avoid overdrawing, and that is a sound reason. A spending limit speaks to both worries: it caps what a thief can reach and what a person can overspend.

## Acceptance criteria
- A card has a **type**: credit, debit or prepaid. It is asked when adding the card, with credit as the default; cards already saved are credit until the user says otherwise. Catalog cards carry their type, so a known debit card is not asked.
- When the user adds a card as debit, one short note appears on that screen, once, and can be dismissed. It says what matters here, not what to buy: debit cards usually have no warranty or purchase protection, so WarrantyCat will not add any for purchases on this card, and fraud protection is generally weaker than on credit because the money leaves your account first.
- Below the note, set apart and clearly labelled, is a **Tip** (the owner's wording): "Tip: Setting a spending limit per month can help with staying out of debt." It is a tip about a habit, not a warning about the card, so it is styled differently from the note (a lighter, friendly look, not the warning colour). The fraud side of a limit (it caps what a thief can reach) is not added to the tip; it stays short. No product is named, and nothing is recommended as better than the user's own choice.
- It is a note, never a block: the user can add the card and carry on, and a debit card works like any other in every other way.
- For a debit or prepaid card the benefit review (S3.3.2) offers nothing to confirm, and says why. A user who knows their card has a benefit can still add it by hand.
- The standing bell notice (S3.3.2) counts only credit cards, and is switched off for a user who has no credit card and says so ("I don't use credit cards"); the card list does not show the "No policy added" warning on a debit or prepaid card, and shows "Debit: no warranty benefits" instead.
- On the purchase screens, the notice that a card's benefits are unconfirmed is not shown for a debit or prepaid card.
- Wording is plain, short and not preachy, and is checked against the card networks' and the regulator's current published rules before it ships (for example, the limits on a customer's loss for fraud differ for debit and credit, and for a prepaid card depend on the issuer and on whether the card is registered). Nothing is stated as a rule that the user's own card agreement could change.

## Not in this story
- Recommending a card, an issuer or a prepaid product, or linking to one. WarrantyCat gives no financial advice and has no commercial relationship with any card.
- Judging the user's choice. Prepaid cards vary widely in the protection they give, so they are described, never steered to.
