# S3.4.5 Plan types are shared data, whoever sells them

**Feature:** F3.4 Plans and insurance you hold  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M8 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S3.4.2 (plans); S3.2.1 (party and versioned policy model); S6.1.1 (candidate submission); S6.6.1 (contributed warranty documents)  
**Found by:** Steve, 2026-10-27, from the 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty page and sample contract. Widened 2026-10-28 (Steve): stores and makers sell plans too.

As a **user** who bought a plan, from a warranty company, a store or the maker, I want that plan's type (such as 2-10's Systems and Pinnacle, a store's 2-year plan or ASUS Premium Care) to be known, so adding mine takes a pick instead of re-typing what the seller already publishes.

## Why this matters
Anyone can sell a plan. A warranty company sells a few named plans with published terms: 2-10 sells two, Systems ($39.99 a month, heating and air, electrical and plumbing, up to $5,000 for heating and air repair or replacement) and Pinnacle ($59.99 a month, adds kitchen and laundry appliances, up to $2,000 per appliance). A store sells its protection plan at the register (Geek Squad, Home Depot Protection Plan), and a maker sells its own (ASUS Premium Care, AppleCare). Each user's contract then fills in their own plan number, dates, price paid and address. The plan type is the same for everyone; the contract is theirs. The reference data already holds this shape for stores and brands (a shared party with versioned policies), so a plan type is the same idea for any company that sells one.

**Who sells it is not who pays.** A store usually sells a plan that another company administers. The store takes the money and puts the plan on the receipt; claims go to the administrator. A plan type therefore records both, so "Make a claim" (S3.4.2, S3.4.8) opens the right company's phone and site.

## Acceptance criteria
- **A plan type belongs to the company that sells it, whatever its role** (store, brand, warranty provider, or several). It is not limited to the "warranty provider" role: a store or a maker with no such role can still carry plan types, and adding a plan type does not require changing the company's roles.
- **Sold by and serviced by.** A plan type records the seller and, separately, the company that services claims (the same company when it sells its own plan). The administrator is a shared company in its own right, with its claim phone and site. Where the receipt or contract names the administrator, the user's plan records it; where it does not, the plan type's value is used and marked as from the company's record.
- **A plan type records:** name, what it covers by category or named item, per-item or per-category limits, exclusions, waiting period (S3.4.6), usual service fee range, how it renews, how it is billed, the published price as a note (never used as the user's price), and a link to the company's page or sample contract as the source.
- **Price tiers.** A plan's price often depends on the item's price (a 2-year plan at one price for items up to a limit, another above it). A plan type holds its tiers as notes with their source; the user's own price always comes from their receipt or contract.
- **Cancel with the item.** A plan type can record a rule such as "cancel within 30 days for a full refund", and that a plan sold with an item is refunded when the item is returned (the behaviour of S2.4.13, S5.4.20), so the guidance can say so before the user returns an item.
- **Versioned like other policies** (S3.2.1): a change takes effect on a date, the earlier version stays for contracts signed under it, and a user's plan keeps pointing at the version they bought.
- **Choosing a plan type** on the plan form fills coverage notes, exclusions, waiting period, fee range, renewal terms and who services it from the record, marked as coming from the company's record. The user's own contract values (dates, price, contract number, service fee actually charged) always win over the record. A plan read from a receipt line (S2.4.7) is matched to a plan type by seller and name where there is one.
- **Not on the list?** The user types it as their own plan type, private to the account and queued as a candidate (S6.1.1), as for a store or brand that is not listed. Nothing a user types here is shown to anyone else until an operator verifies it.
- **Sample contracts help curation.** A sample or blank plan agreement uploaded by a user, or found by an operator, is read the same way as a contract (S3.4.2) and offered as a proposed plan type with its quotes. An operator confirms it from the company's own page, as for return policies (S3.2.5): the source address is required and nothing is saved from the reading alone.
- **Where it shows.** A "Plans they sell" section on the company's page, whether it is a store, a brand or a warranty company, and on the operator's Stores and Brands pages, each plan type with where it stands (none, unconfirmed, confirmed). The warranty-company page (S3.4.4) lists the same section.

## Out of scope
Benefits that come with a store membership or a card (an extra year of coverage, purchase protection) are not plans: they are benefits of the card or membership, as in F3.3.

## Open questions
- Whether a plan type is a new table or a kind of the existing versioned policy. This story leans to the latter if the fields fit, and a new table if per-item limits or price tiers make it awkward.
- Whether the first seed is hand-entered for a handful of large providers (2-10, American Home Shield, Choice, First American, Frontdoor) and the common store and maker plans (Geek Squad, Home Depot, Best Buy, AppleCare, Premium Care), or left to users and operators to grow.
- Whether the administrator can differ by state or by date for the same plan type. Treat it as versioned data until a case shows otherwise.
