# S3.4.5 A company's plan types are shared data

**Feature:** F3.4 Plans and insurance you hold  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M8 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S3.4.2 (plans); S3.2.1 (party and versioned policy model); S6.1.1 (candidate submission); S6.6.1 (contributed warranty documents)  
**Found by:** Steve, 2026-10-27, from the 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty page and sample contract.

As a **user** who bought a plan from a warranty company other people also use, I want the company's plan types (such as 2-10's Systems and Pinnacle) to be known, so adding mine takes a pick instead of re-typing what the company already publishes.

## Why this matters
A warranty company sells a few named plans with published terms. 2-10 sells two: Systems ($39.99 a month, heating and air, electrical and plumbing, up to $5,000 for heating and air repair or replacement) and Pinnacle ($59.99 a month, adds kitchen and laundry appliances, up to $2,000 per appliance). Each user's contract then fills in their own plan number, dates, price paid and address. The plan type is the same for everyone; the contract is theirs. The reference data already holds this shape for stores and brands (a shared party with versioned policies), so a plan type is the same idea for warranty providers.

## Acceptance criteria
- **A plan type** belongs to a warranty provider (shared party) and records: name, what it covers by category or named item, per-item or per-category limits, exclusions, waiting period (S3.4.6), usual service fee range, how it renews, how it is billed, the published price as a note (never used as the user's price), and a link to the company's page or sample contract as the source.
- **Versioned like other policies** (S3.2.1): a change takes effect on a date, the earlier version stays for contracts signed under it, and a user's plan keeps pointing at the version they bought.
- **Choosing a plan type** on the plan form fills coverage notes, exclusions, waiting period, fee range and renewal terms from it, marked as coming from the company's record. The user's own contract values (dates, price, contract number, service fee actually charged) always win over the record.
- **Not on the list?** The user types it as their own plan type, private to the account and queued as a candidate (S6.1.1), as for a store or brand that is not listed. Nothing a user types here is shown to anyone else until an operator verifies it.
- **Sample contracts help curation.** A sample or blank plan agreement uploaded by a user, or found by an operator, is read the same way as a contract (S3.4.2) and offered as a proposed plan type with its quotes. An operator confirms it from the company's own page, as for return policies (S3.2.5): the source address is required and nothing is saved from the reading alone.
- **The Admin Stores page's counterpart** lists warranty providers with their plan types and where each stands (none, unconfirmed, confirmed).

## Open questions
- Whether a plan type is a new table or a kind of the existing versioned policy. This story leans to the latter if the fields fit, and a new table if per-item limits make it awkward.
- Whether the first seed is hand-entered for a handful of large providers (2-10, American Home Shield, Choice, First American, Frontdoor) or left to users and operators to grow.
