# S3.4.14 A plan bought after the item

**Feature:** F3.4 Plans and insurance you hold  
**Phase:** v2  
**Milestone:** M8 (proposed)  
**Depends on:** S3.4.2 (plans); S3.4.10 (add from the item); S3.4.6 (waiting period)  
**Found by:** Steve, 2026-11-03.

As a **user** who added a plan days or weeks after buying the item (AppleCare within its enrolment window, a plan from the maker's site, a home warranty bought a year into owning a house), I want the plan's dates to be the plan's own, not the item's, so reminders and "am I covered today" are right.

## Why this matters
Receipt-borne plans start on the purchase or delivery date (S3.4.3). A plan bought later has its own start, its own receipt or confirmation email, and sometimes a waiting period. Defaulting it to the item's purchase date would make coverage appear earlier than it is, at exactly the moment a claim depends on it.

## Acceptance criteria
- The S3.4.10 sheet asks **"When did the plan start?"** with the choices *Same day as the purchase*, *On delivery*, *Another date* (a picker; defaults to today when chosen via "bought later"). The date is never defaulted silently to the purchase date when the plan's own document carries a different one.
- A plan document that is dated later than the item's purchase is read as bought-later; the start is the document's stated effective date, else the order date.
- The plan keeps its own proof (receipt or confirmation email) as its proof of purchase. The item's receipt is not attached to it.
- A plan whose start is in the future shows "Starts <date>" and status *not yet started*; the item does not count it as coverage until then.
- A waiting period (S3.4.6) counts from the plan's own start.
- Where the plan text states an enrolment window ("within 60 days of purchase") and the person's start is outside it, show the quote as a note ("Your plan says enrolment is within 60 days; your dates are 75 days apart. Check the contract."). Not a block, not a verdict.
- An end date counted from a term (S3.4.2) says it was counted, from which start.

## Out of scope
A reminder or nudge to buy a plan before an enrolment window closes (that is advice to buy).
