# S3.5.4 When vehicle coverage starts, and who it follows

**Feature:** F3.5 Vehicles
**Phase:** v2
**Depends on:** S3.5.1; S3.5.3; S3.1.8 (transferability); the start-date rules that already exist (a start the owner has to confirm)

As a **user**, I want a vehicle's coverage to start on the right day and to say whether it still applies to me when I bought the vehicle used, so I do not count from the wrong date or assume coverage I do not have.

## Why
Factory coverage starts on the day the vehicle was first put in service, not the day I bought it. A second owner may have the remainder, a different term, or nothing. Certified pre-owned programs add their own coverage with their own start.

## Acceptance criteria
- **New vehicle:** coverage starts on the in-service date. Until the person enters it, the start is the purchase date and the coverage is marked "start date to confirm", using the existing confirm-the-start mechanism; the confirmed date replaces it
- **Used vehicle:** the factory coverages start on the **original** in-service date, which the owner may not know. If entered, the date arithmetic runs from it and the used vehicle shows the coverage left. If not known, factory coverages are not proposed with an invented date: the page says "Add the date this vehicle was first put in service to see what factory coverage is left" and the coverage is created "start not known" without an end date
- The miles side of a factory coverage counts from the odometer's zero, so a used vehicle's current reading (S3.5.2) is what it is measured against; the odometer at purchase is not subtracted
- **Certified pre-owned and dealer-added coverage** is its own coverage with its own start date and its own start odometer (the reading at certification). It is never merged into the factory ones
- Each coverage records whether it can be passed on (S3.1.8's transferability), and the page says it in plain words for a used vehicle: "Original owner only. Not transferable. The factory warranty does not apply to you" or "Transfers to the next owner". Not stated stays "not stated"
- A vehicle bought used is not told it is covered by the factory warranty because the VIN decodes to a make and year; only a document or the person's entry creates the coverage
- A change of owner by sale is not built here (S3.5.5 and the item-given-away story deal with what leaves with the vehicle); this story only records and shows the terms
- All wording describes what the document says; whether a maker honors a transfer is the maker's

## Notes
- The existing "start rule" values (registration, installation) gain "in service". A coverage whose start the owner has not confirmed is listed in the item's to-dos.
- Do not read a model year as an in-service date. A 2024 model can be sold in 2023.
