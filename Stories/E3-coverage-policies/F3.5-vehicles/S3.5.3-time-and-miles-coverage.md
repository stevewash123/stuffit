# S3.5.3 Time-and-miles coverage: whichever comes first

**Feature:** F3.5 Vehicles
**Phase:** v2
**Depends on:** S3.5.1; S3.5.2 (odometer readings); S3.1.1 (coverage); S3.2.8 (component coverages); S3.1.6 (earliest and latest end)

As a **user**, I want a vehicle's warranty shown as the date **and** the miles it ends at, and which of the two will probably come first, so I do not find out at the repair counter that the miles ran out last spring.

## Why
"5 years or 60,000 miles, whichever comes first" is the normal shape of vehicle coverage, and a vehicle carries several at once (bumper-to-bumper, powertrain, corrosion, emissions, hybrid or electric battery, roadside). This is the one new piece of core logic in the vehicle feature.

## Acceptance criteria
- A coverage can carry a **mile limit** beside its date limit. Either side can be absent: "5 years, unlimited miles" has a date and no miles; "60,000 miles, no time limit" has miles and no date. A coverage with neither is not a vehicle coverage
- A vehicle can have any number of coverages, each named by what it covers (the component field from S3.2.8: Bumper-to-bumper, Powertrain, Corrosion, Emissions, Hybrid battery, Roadside), each with its own date and miles
- The item page shows each coverage in plain words, for example: "Powertrain. Until Nov 4, 2031 or 60,000 miles, whichever comes first. About 17,900 miles left (estimated). At your pace the miles run out first, around March 2030."
- The date end is exact. The mile end rests on readings: from the **latest entered reading** the remaining miles are limit minus reading; with a pace (S3.5.2) the date the miles will probably run out is estimated and shown as an estimate
- **Which first** is stated only when there is a pace; without one the page shows both limits and says "Add odometer readings to see which comes first"
- A coverage is **ended by miles** only when an **entered** reading is at or over its mile limit; it then shows as expired like any coverage, with "ended by miles on <reading date>" and the reading that did it. An estimate alone never ends a coverage: when the estimated odometer is over the limit but the last entered reading is under, it says "You have probably passed the limit. Enter your odometer to check" and the coverage stays Active
- A coverage ended by miles keeps its history and can be corrected if the reading was wrong (the correction re-opens it)
- Every miles figure shows the age of the reading it rests on, and when the reading is more than 90 days old says so ("last reading 14 months ago")
- **Reminders** plan on the earlier of the date end and the estimated mile end. When no pace exists they plan on the date end only and the page says the miles side is not being watched. A reminder for a miles end says it is an estimate and asks for a fresh odometer reading
- Home's "ending soon" count and the item's attention level use the same earlier end, labelled estimated when it comes from miles
- A coverage with no miles behaves exactly as today. Appliances are unchanged
- **The warranty reader** fills the mile limit from the document ("5 years or 60,000 miles") with its quote, and leaves it empty rather than guessing when the document gives none; "unlimited miles" is kept as unlimited, not as a number. The person can correct every field
- The same fields are in the coverage edit form, the claim guidance ("Something broke" shows the date and the miles check), the items export, and the proof the person sees; none of them promise a repair

## Notes
- Stored as a mile limit on the coverage and a mile start (0 for a factory coverage; the install odometer for a part, S3.5.6). The vehicle's odometer unit applies.
- Federal emissions coverage, battery coverage and the like are simply separate coverages the person or the reader adds; WarrantyCat does not know them by itself.
- Not legal advice: it describes what the document says. What the maker will do is up to the maker.
