# S3.5.6 Part warranties that run by miles from the day it was installed

**Feature:** F3.5 Vehicles
**Phase:** v2
**Depends on:** S3.5.2; S3.5.3 (time-and-miles coverage); S3.5.5 (parts on a vehicle)

As a **user**, I want a part's warranty to count from the day and the odometer reading when it was installed, so a tire's "50,000 miles" means miles on those tires, not miles on the vehicle.

## Why
A set of tires bought at 38,000 miles with a 50,000-mile treadwear warranty runs out at 88,000 on the odometer. A battery may be free to replace for 36 months and then prorated for 36 more. A part's clock starts when it goes on, not when it was bought, and its miles are counted from where the odometer was then.

## Acceptance criteria
- A part's coverage can carry **installed on** (a date) and **odometer at install** (a reading on the parent vehicle's odometer). With them, its mile limit is counted from the install reading: the mile end on the vehicle's odometer is install reading plus the limit
- Until **installed on** is entered, the date starts from the purchase date and the coverage is marked "start date to confirm" (the existing confirm-the-start mechanism); the miles side shows "Add the odometer when it was installed to watch the miles"
- A part with no vehicle (not linked) can still have a date-only warranty; the miles side needs the vehicle's odometer and says so
- Everything from S3.5.3 applies to a part: both limits shown, which comes first when there is a pace, ended by miles only on an entered reading, estimates labelled, reminders planned on the earlier end
- **Two-stage and prorated warranties** are separate coverages on the part: "Free replacement" (36 months) and "Prorated" (to 72 months). A prorated coverage is labelled **Prorated: you pay a share** on the item page, in claim guidance and in the list, and is never shown as full coverage. WarrantyCat shows the stated terms and does not calculate the share
- The warranty reader fills the mile limit, the time limit and the prorated stage from the document with quotes, and leaves what the document does not say empty
- Moving a part to another vehicle (winter tires) asks for a new install date and reading, keeps the earlier ones as history, and counts the miles for each stretch on that vehicle's odometer. Unlinking a part leaves its date-only coverage
- When the vehicle is deleted or disposed and the person keeps the parts, each part keeps its dates and its last known mile end, marked "miles no longer watched"
- Everything is in the export; nothing promises a replacement

## Notes
- Mileage on tires is usually a treadwear promise with conditions (rotation, pressure, proof of service); conditions are shown as stated by the document, not judged.
- Service records that keep a part's warranty valid (rotation receipts) are shown as "asks for" by S3.1.9, and logging them is the maintenance feature (later).
