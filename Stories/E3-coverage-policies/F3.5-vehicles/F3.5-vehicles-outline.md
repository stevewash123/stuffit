# F3.5 Vehicles: warranties, parts, maintenance and repairs (OUTLINE, decisions recorded 2026-11-05)

**Epic:** E3 Coverage and policies (with pieces in E4 reminders and E5 claims)
**Phase:** draft; scope decisions below are settled, nothing built
**Replaces and widens:** S2.7.15 (VIN gives the rest), which stays as the VIN story.

**Vehicles, not cars.** One "vehicle" type covers cars, motorcycles, boats, RVs and the like until a real difference in behavior turns up; then that difference gets its own discussion (Steve, 2026-11-05).

A car is not an appliance. Its warranty runs by **time and miles, whichever comes first**; it carries a **list of parts** that have their own warranties; and its owner needs a **record of maintenance and repairs**, partly because some coverage asks for service records. Build on what exists (items, coverages, plans, reminders, claims, paperwork reading) and add only what a car needs.

## A. The vehicle
- **V1 Add a vehicle.** Its own form, not the appliance form: nickname, year, make, model, trim, VIN, new or used, date bought or put in service, price, odometer at purchase. A vehicle is an item with a type.
- **V2 VIN and door-sticker photo.** Decode make, model, year, plant; check digit. (This is S2.7.15.)
- **V3 Odometer readings.** A dated log. Entered by hand, or read from a service invoice or a dash photo. Gives a miles-per-year pace.

## B. Vehicle coverage
- **V4 Time-and-miles coverage.** "5 years or 60,000 miles, whichever first": bumper-to-bumper, powertrain, corrosion, emissions, hybrid or EV battery, roadside. Shows the date and the miles, says which will probably come first at the owner's pace, and warns as either nears. This is the new core logic.
- **V5 When the clock starts and who it follows.** Starts at in-service date, not purchase date. Used and certified pre-owned cars keep the remaining factory coverage. Second-owner terms. (Ties to S3.1.8 transfer.)
- **V6 Vehicle service contracts.** The extended plan from the dealer or a third party: deductible, exclusions, limits, per-visit terms. Reuse the plans feature (F3.4), with miles added.
- **V7 Auto insurance on the vehicle.** Link the existing insurance policy to the car: deductible, claim number to call. Optional.
- **V8 Read vehicle paperwork.** Warranty booklet, window sticker, service contract, bill of sale, into the same reading pipeline, with "not stated" kept as not stated.

## C. Parts as items
- **V9 Parts on a vehicle.** A vehicle links to a list of part items: tires, battery, brakes, stereo, remote start, lift kit, accessories. A part is an ordinary item with a **parent**, so it keeps its own warranty, receipt and reminders. (The same link would later serve appliances with parts.)
- **V10 Part warranties by miles from install.** Tires (treadwear miles), battery (free replacement then prorated). The clock starts at the **install date and odometer**, not the purchase date. Prorating shown as a share, never as full coverage.
- **V11 Parts follow the car, or don't.** Selling the car: the parts go with it, and what that means for each warranty is shown. A part moved to another vehicle (winter tires) keeps its own record.
- **V12 Help text on aftermarket parts and warranties.** Plain, brief, not legal advice: what the document says, and that the maker decides in practice.

## D. Maintenance
- **V13 A maintenance schedule.** Oil, rotation, brake fluid, belts, by miles or by time, from the owner's manual or the make and model; "next due" estimated from the pace.
- **V14 Log a service.** Date, odometer, shop, what was done, cost, the invoice attached through the normal paperwork reader. The odometer reading goes into V3.
- **V15 Maintenance reminders.** By date and by miles; asks for the odometer when needed; never nags if the person does not track miles.
- **V16 Service records that keep coverage.** Where a warranty or contract asks for service records (S3.1.9 proof), the vehicle page says "keep your service records" and shows what is on file.

## E. Repairs, claims and recalls
- **V17 Repair log.** What failed, which coverage paid (factory, contract, part warranty, or the owner), cost, shop, repair order. Linked to the claim log (F5.2) and to Money recovered.
- **V18 Something broke, for a car.** Which coverage fits (powertrain or bumper-to-bumper or the part), the miles check, who to call (dealer, contract company, roadside).
- **V19 Repeat repairs and lemon-law notes.** Count the same defect, days out of service, miles at each repair; show the state's window as information. Describes records, gives no legal advice.
- **V20 Recalls.** Open recalls by VIN from the public source, with the lookup date; notify when a new one appears. A recall repair is free, so say so.

## F. Cost and the whole picture
- **V21 Cost of ownership.** Purchase, maintenance, repairs, minus money recovered; per year and per mile.
- **V22 Vehicle history for a sale.** One export of service and repair history, the coverage left, the parts and their warranties.
- **V23 Vehicle on Home.** Next service due, coverage ending by date or miles, open recalls.

## Suggested order
1. **Slice 1, the vehicle and its coverage:** V1, V3, V4, V5, V9, V10. This is where the value is and where the new logic (miles) lives.
2. **Slice 2, maintenance:** V13 to V16.
3. **Slice 3, repairs:** V17, V18, V8. (Recalls V20 are a separate, deferred story.)
4. **Later:** V2, V6, V7, V11, V12, V19, V21 to V23.

## Decisions (Steve, 2026-11-05)
1. **Vehicles, not car-specific.** Split a type out only when its behavior really differs, and discuss it first.
2. **Miles are entered by hand.** If service-record documents are read later (V14), the odometer on them can be mined as a second source; the person's entry stays the authority, and a mined reading is shown as read from the document.
3. **A part is always an item**, with a parent vehicle (V9).
4. **Recalls (V20) are a separate story, fine but deferred.** Not in the first slices.
5. **A mileage-based coverage shows both the date and the miles**, and says which will probably come first (V4).

Still open: nothing blocking Slice 1.
