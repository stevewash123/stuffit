# F6.9 Model-level warranty terms from owners' documents

**Epic:** E6 Curation & Learning  
**Phase:** future (build the read-only parts first)

Some brands (A. O. Smith, appliance makers) set the warranty by model, and the real term only shows on the unit's paperwork or behind a serial-number lookup. We cannot read that from the brand's site. Owners' uploaded warranty documents (F6.6) are the source: each one is a single observation, observations are grouped by model line, and an operator promotes a clear group to a model-level policy.

## Terms behind a lookup
Two situations look alike to us: the term is not on any page we can read.
- **Brands whose warranty differs by model** (A. O. Smith, many appliance makers): a general page gives a range or a floor, and the real term depends on the model.
- **Brands and stores that need data to look the terms up**: a serial number, a reference number, a model number, an order number or the purchase email entered into a form on their site. Their terms exist but cannot be saved by the page saver or read by the AI.

We record that fact on the company (S6.9.6) instead of presenting a general term as the answer. The general policy stays as the floor, owners are told what to check and what the lookup needs, and the owner's own document is the source of the real term (S6.9.1).

## Principles
- A document is an **observation**, never a policy. Nothing is published from one upload.
- The brand's general policy stays the floor. Model-level terms use what already exists: a manufacturer policy limited to a product-tree node, listed model numbers or a purchase window (S3.2.8). No new policy schema.
- Weight and bands come from the evidence ladder (S3.2.5), matching from S6.1.4. This feature supplies the model key those stories leave open (S6.2.3: "split by model key").
- Shared data follows the standing rule (concept.md, S6.6.3): only the extracted fact is shared, never the owner, the serial number, order details or the file.
