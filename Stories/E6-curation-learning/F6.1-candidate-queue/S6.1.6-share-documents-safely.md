# S6.1.6 Share uploaded warranty documents safely to improve matching

**Feature:** F6.1 Candidate policy queue  
**Phase:** MVP  
**Milestone:** M5  
**Depends on:** S7.2.2; S6.1.4 (claims follow the same choice)  
**Renumbered:** was S6.1.3 "Sharing is the default"; S6.1.3 is now only the card terms research agent (2026-11-04)  

As a **member**, I want my uploaded warranty documents shared anonymously with the system by default, with personal details always redacted and shown to me first, so the system's coverage matching gets better without exposing my private information.

## Acceptance criteria
1. The membership terms say plainly that uploaded warranty documents are shared anonymously by default to improve matching and to verify existing records
2. When I upload a document, the system scans it for personal information such as names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, order or contract numbers, serial numbers or VINs, and payment details
3. If nothing personal is found, the document is shared automatically with no prompt
4. If personal information is found, it is redacted automatically, and I see a preview with each redaction highlighted before anything is shared
5. From that preview I can share the redacted version or skip sharing. If I skip, the document stays private in my account and still works fully for my own tracking
6. Receipts, registration cards, and extended warranties or service contracts with custom terms always go through the redaction check
7. A setting lets me turn off all sharing, and it applies to future uploads
8. I can see a list of what I've shared and withdraw any item
9. Shared data never carries my identity or account link

## Replaces
The 2026-10-26 rule: "always shared, no checkbox, no opt-out; extracted facts only, never the document." Reversed 2026-11-04 because an opt-out may be legally required, and because redaction was missing. Sharing is still on by default, but now has automatic redaction with a preview (AC 2 to 6), a skip choice, an off switch (AC 7) and withdraw (AC 8). A redacted copy of the document may be shared, not only extracted facts.

Before that: the Share checkbox (default on), the account setting Contribute to shared data, and per-submission withdraw.

## Decisions that follow from the story
- **Facts follow the same choice.** The extracted facts that become claims (S6.1.4: store and brand names, aliases, return windows, warranty terms) are also sharing. Sharing off, or "skip" on a document, means no claim is written from it. Facts carry no personal data, so they need no redaction, but they obey the setting
- **Turning sharing off is forward-looking only** (AC 7). What was already shared stays until the member withdraws it (AC 8); claim counts drop when a withdrawn item's claim row goes
- **Receipts, registration cards and custom-term contracts always go through the check** (AC 6), even when a scan finds nothing, so they are never auto-shared on a scan miss alone. Receipts remain the most personal; whether a receipt's redacted image is ever worth sharing, versus only its facts, is open
- **Redaction is a gate, not a courtesy.** A document is never shared if the scan did not run or failed; it stays private and the member is told
- **Anonymous means no link** (AC 9): the shared copy and any claim rows carry no account id, owner, upload date to the minute, or filename. An internal distinct-account count may be kept (S6.1.4), never shown or exported

## Deferred concept: opt-in data sales to lower the price (not in scope yet)
As an experiment, StuffIt may offer members the chance to opt in to the sale of aggregate, anonymous data, such as product failure rates by brand and category. The money would go toward lowering the subscription price for everyone.

- Off by default; members only take part if they opt in. This is separate from the sharing for matching above
- The offer explains exactly what would be sold, with a concrete example, and that only groups above a minimum size are included, never individual records
- Members can see the payoff, for example "if X% opt in, the price drops for everyone," along with progress toward that goal
- Opting out is one toggle, takes effect right away, and carries no penalty
- StuffIt publishes regular updates on what kinds of data were sold, to what kinds of buyers, and how much the price dropped
- Before launch, the plan and wording get a privacy attorney's review
