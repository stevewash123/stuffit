# S5.2.1 How much run-around did the warranty give you?

**Feature:** F5.2 Claim log  
**Phase:** v3 (future; not scheduled)  
**Milestone:** unassigned  
**Depends on:** F5.2 claim log (a claim recorded with its outcome); S3.2.5 (policy evidence); S3.2.8 (policies and their components)  

As a **user who made a warranty claim**, I want to say how hard it was to get the warranty honored, so the next person can see what the policy is worth in practice, not just what it says on paper.

## Why
A warranty on paper and a warranty in practice are different things. Forums are full of "even with a warranty, expect a lot of run-around exercising it" (found 2026-10-06 in Steve's reading of refrigerator warranty threads), and a long compressor term is worth little if getting it honored costs weeks and labor charges. That experience is exactly what StuffIt's users hold and nobody else collects in one place, tied to a specific brand, line and component.

## Idea (to refine when it is scheduled)
- After a claim is logged and closed, ask a few plain questions: did they honor it, how long from first contact to fix, how many contacts it took, what you paid out of pocket (labor, shipping, trip charge), and a friction rating from "easy" to "a fight".
- Tie each answer to the policy and component that applied (the compressor term of a Samsung refrigerator, say), and to the model if known.
- Show a policy's friction as a summary ("12 claims: 8 honored within a month, typical cost to the owner $0, 3 needed 4 or more contacts") next to its terms, only once there are enough independent claims to say anything.

## Guardrails
- It is a person's account, not a fact about the maker: label it that way, show counts, never a bare score from one claim.
- Private by default; only the answers (not the person, the receipt or the serial number) are shared, as for crowd-sourced policy data (S3.2.5).
- Resistant to abuse: one rating per claim, per account; claims need a real item on the account; no free text shown to others until reviewed.
- Neutral wording: it must not read as advice to buy or avoid a brand. The product shows what owners reported.

## Not in this story
Anything about how StuffIt helps someone press a claim (E5 F5.1); this is only the record of how it went.
