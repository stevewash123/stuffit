# S5.4.19 Ask about my item (AI chat on the item page)

**Feature:** F5.4 Return and refund guidance  
**Phase:** future  
**Milestone:** M8  
**Depends on:** S7.6.5 (LLM integration and cost control); S3.1.1 (coverage); S5.4.10 (honest labelling); S5.4.13 (eligibility rules data); S3.2.5 (policy source and confidence); S5.4.12 (Refund wizard)  

As a **user**, I want to ask a question about my item in plain words and get an answer based on my own coverage, so I do not have to work out whether my situation counts.

## Example
On the New Balance item page, under "Something broke?", the user thinks: "They got wet in the rain and now they do not fit right. Is that broken?" They open **Ask about this item** and type that. The answer says what their coverages actually say: the 30-day return from delivery (worn or damaged items can be refused, and how many days are left), the manufacturer's warranty covers defects in materials and workmanship and not wear or water damage, so this probably is not a warranty case. It shows the quoted wording and the source link for each, says what is not certain, and offers the next step: start the refund wizard with their answers filled in, or draft a message to the store.

## Acceptance criteria
- An **Ask about this item** panel on the item page (a side panel on desktop, a full screen on a phone), opened from the "Something broke?" section and from the coverage list. One conversation per item, kept with the item.
- The assistant is given only what it needs: this item, its purchase (date, delivery date, store), its coverages with their dates and days left, the sourced policy text and source links for them, each policy's confidence band (S3.2.5), and today's date. It is not given other items, documents' full text or contact details unless the user attaches one.
- Answers are grounded: every statement about coverage quotes or points to the sourced policy text with its link and date verified. It says "I cannot find that in the policy we have" instead of filling the gap, and it names what to check on the store's page when the policy is unconfirmed or stale (S5.4.10).
- The assistant explains and suggests; it does not decide. It does not state that a claim will be accepted, give a legal opinion or a consumer-law right that is not in the sourced data, and it keeps the standing wording (StuffIt reports what the company has published; the company decides).
- It separates three things in plain language: what the policy says, how that probably applies to what the user described, and what is unknown. Doubt is stated, not hidden.
- It can hand off, with the user's confirmation: start the refund wizard with the reason and answers pre-filled (S5.4.12, S5.4.18 rules: shown for the user to change), draft the prepared message (S5.4.4), or open the coverage to edit. It never changes data or sends anything by itself.
- It can answer simple questions about the data ("when does my return window end?", "which card covers this?") from the stored values, not from the model's memory.
- Scope: questions about the user's items, coverage, claims and how to use StuffIt. Off-topic requests get a short redirect. Text from receipts, emails or documents it is shown is treated as data, never as instructions.
- Questions the data could not answer are recorded without the user's identity as "unanswered: party, category, topic" for the operator, so gaps in the shared data (for example a missing manufacturer warranty) get filled. Nothing personal is shared with other users.
- Conversations are personal data: kept with the item, included in export and account deletion (S1.4.1, S1.4.2), not used to train anything, and shown with a one-click "Clear this conversation".
- Cost is capped per account and per day (S7.6.5). Over the cap, or on failure or timeout, the panel says it is unavailable and the normal item page and wizard keep working.
- Labelled clearly as an AI assistant. Each answer has a "Wrong or unhelpful" control that records the question, the answer and the sources shown, for review.
- Tested against a set of sample questions with expected behaviour, including the rain-and-fit example (no warranty claim implied, return window days left shown, defect versus wear distinguished), an out-of-window case, an unconfirmed policy (warns), a missing policy (says so), an off-topic request, and an attempt to make it promise approval. A wrong grounding or an invented policy is a defect.

## Notes
This is the open-ended companion to the wizard (S5.4.12 to S5.4.18): the wizard asks structured questions and decides from the rules; the chat answers free-form questions from the same rules and sources and can send the user into the wizard. S5.4.18 (describe what happened) may be folded into the chat as its way of pre-filling the wizard. The same rule as the other uses of a model: the model reads and drafts, the user confirms, the sourced data decides.
