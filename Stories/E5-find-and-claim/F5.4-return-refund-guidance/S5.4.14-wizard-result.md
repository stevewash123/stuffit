# S5.4.14 The result: what applies, what does not, and how

**Feature:** F5.4 Return and refund guidance  
**Phase:** MVP candidate  
**Milestone:** QA  
**Depends on:** S5.4.12; S5.4.13; S5.4.10 (Honest labelling of guidance)  

As a **user**, I want the answer ranked, with the reason for each, and the exact steps for the one I choose.

## Acceptance criteria
- API: `POST /api/items/{id}/claim-guidance` with the reason and the answers; returns each coverage as `applies`, `maybe` or `does_not_apply`, a one-line reason in plain words, the days left (using the earliest end if the date is approximate, S3.1.6), the steps, and what to have ready
- Evaluation happens on the server from S5.4.13 so the app and any later client agree; the browser does not carry the rules
- Ranked: routes that fit the reason and are about to close come first; the top one is marked "Best option" with the reason it is best
- "Does not apply" routes stay visible and say why ("A manufacturer warranty does not cover changing your mind") so the user trusts the answer
- Warns when taking one route closes another (S5.4.5): for example returning the item ends the maker warranty, or the card benefit needs the maker's denial first
- Steps are numbered and can be ticked off; links open the seller, maker or card portal; phone numbers are tap-to-call; "have ready" is a checklist (S5.4.3)
- Each route shows source and date verified (S5.4.10); the standing wording says the company decides and the page never says a claim will be approved
- Deadlines use the real last day with shipping or drop-off time when known (S5.4.6)
- When nothing applies, say so, show the nearest options (for example ask the maker about goodwill, or the seller about a courtesy return) clearly labelled as requests, not rights
- Integration tests cover each reason against sample policies, the unknown case, the date-approximate case and cross-account isolation

## Status
Mock built: ranked options with applies / does not apply, steps and a have-ready list on sample data.
