# S5.5.3 Accessibility tags and filter on return stories

**Feature:** F5.5 Returns that work for people with disabilities
**Phase:** future, tentative, speculative
**Milestone:** M8 or later (proposed)
**Depends on:** S5.5.2

As a **user** who found a return hard because of a disability, I want to mark my story as accessibility-related, and as a reader I want to filter for stories like mine, so I can find out which stores and routes actually work.

## Acceptance criteria
- When posting a story (S5.5.2), the user can tick **Accessibility-related** and, optionally, pick what applies: *getting into or around the store*, *the line or wait*, *the counter or desk*, *carrying the item*, *phone-only or hearing*, *forms or screens*, *deadlines*, *a store accommodation was offered*, *a store accommodation was refused*, *other*. The user does not say what their disability is and is never asked.
- The tag is optional and about the experience, not the person. It is not stored on the account and is not used for anything but this display.
- Readers can filter a store's stories by **Accessibility-related** and by one of the above. Counts show on the store page ("5 accessibility-related stories; 3 mention a store accommodation").
- A story that mentions an accommodation shows what was asked and what the store did, in plain words, to help others ask. A refusal is shown as the author's report, with the same "experiences vary" note.
- Stories tagged **accommodation offered** by several people can feed a per-seller note (S5.5.4) after an operator reads them.
- Moderation: a tagged story gets the same checks as any other, plus the rule that it must not contain medical details. A reminder in the form says so.
- Not legal advice: a link beside the filter opens a short, sourced "general information about the ADA and returns" page (S5.5.1 question 3).

## Notes
- The flag is also useful research data for S5.5.1: how many stories are tagged, which stores come up, what helps. Aggregate only; it is never sold or shared by person.
- Do not use "ADA" as the tag name in the interface unless we mean the law specifically. "Accessibility" is wider and correct for non-US users.
